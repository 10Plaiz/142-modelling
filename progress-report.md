# Progress report: rebuilding the study around the real gate

**Status: in progress, not ready to submit.** Nothing is committed. This file lists what has changed on disk since commit `262e975`, what is still missing, and the decisions we need from the group.

## Why the study is being rebuilt

Three facts from the group changed the study:

1. **The gate is a sticker check, then an ID tap, at one stop.** The guard checks the parking sticker, then the driver taps a University ID at the **one** reader. A second guard therefore can't open a second lane. It can only pre-check the sticker of the next car while the car ahead taps.
2. **There is no access to the gate or its records.** No timings, gate log or counts can be collected. Every campus input is now a documented estimate. The old placeholder data and the plan to collect field data are gone.
3. **Nobody has seen the line reach the street.** The old proposal claimed spillover happens every morning. That claim is removed. The study now measures how much margin the gate has before the line would reach Pablo Ocampo Sr. Extension.

This follows the recommendation in `combined-study-review.md`: keep Study A's analysis framework and bring in Study B's more realistic gate (two car types, the Lindley check, the stress test, guard-hours). One point in the review no longer holds. It assumed two parallel servers, but with one reader, the second guard works *upstream* of the stop.

## What is done

### Model: `Group8_Parking_Booth_Simulation/parking_sim.py` (rewritten)

- **Configurations:**

  | Configuration | Guard-hours |
  |---|---|
  | A: 1 guard | 12 |
  | B: 2nd guard 07:00–09:00 | 14 |
  | C: 2nd guard 07:00–10:00 | 15 |

- **Activities per car:**
  - sticker check **C**
  - ID tap plus barrier **T**
  - move-up **M**
  - refusal **R**, for the estimated 5% of cars with no sticker, which are turned away
- **One guard:** the car holds the stop for C + T + M, or for C + R if it has no sticker.
- **Second guard on duty:** the car behind the stop is pre-checked at a pre-check spot. It keeps that spot until the stop frees, since there is no buffer between them (a two-station line with blocking). At the stop it then needs only T + M. Guard 2 pre-checks **only when the stop is occupied**. With the stop free, the car drives straight up and guard 1 checks it.
- **Arrivals:** generated by inverting the cumulative rate function (from Study B). Each random input has its own stream: arrivals, type, C, T, M and R. Car *i* is therefore the same car in every configuration **and** every demand level (common random numbers).
- **Measures:**
  - delay in seconds (time in system minus the car's own activity time);
  - maximum queue on the approach (the car at the pre-check spot counts; the car at the stop doesn't);
  - guard utilization over on-duty time;
  - spillover minutes at or above K;
  - supplementary: the morning, 90th-percentile and longest delays, stop occupancy, and guard-hours.
- **Also in the file:**
  - the Lindley recursion and the Pollaczek-Khinchine formula, used as verification benchmarks;
  - two tests of the arrival assumption: uniformity within an interval, and dispersion across days;
  - the fluid-flow cross-check (Euler / RK4), rebuilt as a single-bottleneck M/G/1 model.
- **Removed:** service-time fitting (there are no timings to fit), the M/M/2 benchmark, and all placeholder-data loading.

### Checked so far

- **The two-station hand trace matches the one worked on paper.** Arrivals are at 0, 1 and 2 min, with C = 2 and T = 3. One guard: cars leave at 5, 10 and 15, with delays of 0, 4 and 8. With the pre-check: cars leave at 5, 8 and 11, with delays of 0, 2 and 4.
- **`python parking_sim.py` runs.** It gives 30 replications at the estimated demand:

  | Configuration | Mean delay | Max queue | Guard utilization | Spillover |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | A: 1 guard | 2.6 s | 3.5 cars | 0.058 | 0 min |
  | B: 2nd guard 07–09 | 1.8 s | 3.6 cars | 0.050 | 0 min |
  | C: 2nd guard 07–10 | 1.7 s | 3.6 cars | 0.046 | 0 min |

  With the group's estimates, one guard is nowhere near its limit. A second guard saves under a second per car. Its maximum queue is about the same, and slightly higher, because a car being pre-checked or refused at the pre-check spot counts as being on the approach.

### Real public data for the arrival assumption: `tools/build_arrival_proxy.py` (new)

- **Purpose:** the script extracts real per-vehicle entry times from the public **SFpark** garage transactions (San Francisco Municipal Transportation Agency, 2011–2013). They are used only to test assumption A1: Poisson arrivals with a rate that is constant within each 15-minute interval.
- **Not for campus volumes.** A downtown pay garage has no class-start peak, so it never sets campus volumes.
- **Options:** `--survey` counts entries per garage; `--source` reads a downloaded copy instead of streaming.
- **The source file is 812 MB, and the full download was stopped on purpose.** A survey of the first ~85 MB ranked **Civic Center Garage** first for weekday entries from 07:00 to 10:00, with about 26,500 of them. It is the default choice.
- **`data/proxy_sfpark_entries.csv` has not been built yet** (see open decision 1).
