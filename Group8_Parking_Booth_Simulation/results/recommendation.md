## Recommendation

**Recommendation: keep one guard all day (schedule A).** A second guard is not needed at the estimated demand or within any tested ±20% change to demand or activity times.

- One guard: spillover on 0 of 180 simulated days across 80-120% demand and 80-120% activity times; longest approach queue on any of those days: 3 cars (K = 8).
- B: 2nd guard 07-09 minus A: 1 guard: 0.13 seconds saved per car (paired 95% CI 0.08 to 0.17) for 2 extra scheduled guard-hours.
- C: 2nd guard 07-10 minus A: 1 guard: 0.15 seconds saved per car (paired 95% CI 0.10 to 0.19) for 3 extra scheduled guard-hours.
- C: 2nd guard 07-10 minus B: 2nd guard 07-09: 0.02 seconds saved per car (paired 95% CI 0.01 to 0.03) for 1 extra scheduled guard-hours.
- Morning arrivals (07:00-10:00), B: 2nd guard 07-09 minus A: 1 guard: 0.23 seconds saved per car.
- Morning arrivals (07:00-10:00), C: 2nd guard 07-10 minus A: 1 guard: 0.27 seconds saved per car.
- Morning arrivals (07:00-10:00), C: 2nd guard 07-10 minus B: 2nd guard 07-09: 0.04 seconds saved per car.
- Across the activity-time test, the largest mean delay was 0.67 seconds, and the ranking of schedules did not change.

**When this would change.** One guard first averages at least 1 minute of spillover per day at about 10x the estimated demand, a morning peak of about 250 cars per hour (6.4 min/day). At that level a second guard from 07:00 to 09:00 cuts it to 1.1 min/day. If morning arrivals approach that level, for example through more sticker holders or a class schedule that concentrates arrivals, schedule B becomes worth considering. The same applies if field timings show activities several times longer than estimated.

**Limits.** All inputs are group estimates and the shared-reader layout is assumed; no gate records or field timings were available. The conclusion is therefore conditional on those inputs, but the headroom sweep shows it would take a large error in demand to overturn it.
